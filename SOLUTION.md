## 2. Final Solution: Stability Strategy
**Final Metric Achieved: 4.3% Accuracy**  
My final approach is built upon three core pillars: **search space reduction**, **precise initialization**, and **adaptive step sizing**.

### Key Modifications:
1.  **Architecture (Fixed-B LoRA):** I replaced the standard fully connected layer with a LoRA-based structure where matrix $B$ is a fixed random projector, and only matrix $A$ is trainable. This reduced the number of parameters by a factor of 25 (down to ~2000), allowing the ZO-optimizer to identify a clear signal amidst the noise.
2.  **Initialization (The "Cold Start" Trick):** 
    Instead of standard PyTorch initialization, I implemented a scaled Xavier approach:
    ```python
    nn.init.xavier_uniform_(layer.weight)
    layer.weight.data.mul_(0.01) # Crucial scaling factor
    nn.init.zeros_(layer.bias)
    ```
    This was one of the most critical decisions. The starting loss dropped from **9.0–10.0 to 4.6**, providing a much more stable entry point and preventing immediate gradient explosion.
3.  **Optimizer:** After extensive testing, I chose **Adam** (integrated with SPSA). It outperformed standard momentum and sign-based methods in adapting step sizes under heavily noised gradient estimates.

### What Contributed Most to the Boost:
The most significant improvement came from the **synergy between scaled initialization and the Fixed-B constraint**. The initialization removed the initial "shock" to the model, while the dimensionality reduction enabled Adam to navigate the loss landscape effectively.

## 3. Experiments and Failed Attempts: What Was Left Behind

### The Optimizer "Zoo"
I implemented and tested a wide range of methods: *Momentum, Sign-Momentum, Normalized-Momentum, Sign-Adam, and Normalized-Adam*. 
*   **Result:** Sign-based variants were too aggressive, often "overshooting" local minima due to their fixed update magnitude. Standard Adam proved to be the most balanced and reliable.

### The "Backroll" Technique and Internal Epochs
I experimented with passing through the same batch multiple times within a single optimizer step, implementing a **Backroll** technique (rolling back weights if the batch loss spiked too sharply).
*   **Result:** While this produced an impressively smooth training curve initially, the model quickly **overfitted** to specific batches. This caused the overall validation loss to increase. Attempting to "squeeze" every bit of information out of a single batch in a ZO context turned out to be a trap.

### Augmentations and Normalization
I tested custom ImageNet-style normalization (Mean/Std) for the input images.
*   **Result:** This added only **+0.01%** to the final accuracy. I decided not to complicate the pipeline for such a marginal gain, prioritizing the cleanliness and reproducibility of the baseline solution.

### The Turning Point: Backpropagation as a Diagnostic Tool
My ZO-optimizer was failing to converge. To understand why, I took a detour and trained the model using standard **Backpropagation**.
*   **The Insight:** I discovered that the weight updates (deltas) produced by backprop were nearly **100 times smaller** than those generated by my ZO-optimizer. 
*   **The Conclusion:** My optimizer wasn't just "inaccurate"—it was taking "elephant steps" that effectively blinded the model. This realization directly motivated the shift to **LoRA**, as I needed a way to stabilize the updates by restricting the optimization to a low-dimensional subspace.

### Full LoRA (Trainable A and B)
Attempting to train both LoRA matrices simultaneously resulted in a "mathematical bottleneck." Since both updates are small ($\approx \epsilon$), the resulting weight change was proportional to $\epsilon^2$. This signal was too weak for the ZO-optimizer to distinguish from random noise. Switching to **Fixed-B** (ensuring an $O(\epsilon)$ response) was the turning point for the project.

By stabilizing the initialization and reducing the parameter space through LoRA, I transformed a chaotic search into an optimization process. This stability suggests that the solution is highly scalable: with a larger data budget and more iterations, the model is well-positioned to continue learning and reach even higher performance levels.
